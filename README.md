# Numerical Physics Projects

Collection of numerical physics projects completed at EPFL.

This repository contains five technical reports covering numerical methods applied to problems in plasma physics, nonlinear dynamics, gravitational systems, electrostatics, and wave propagation.

The projects were completed jointly with Pasqual Hernández Riveiro as part of the Numerical Physics course.

All five projects received excellent grades: **6.0/6.0 for Exercises 2–5 and 5.85/6.0 for Exercise 1**.

## Projects

### Exercise 1 — Runaway Electrons in Plasma
Zero-dimensional modelling of runaway-electron dynamics, including analytical solutions and explicit, implicit, and semi-implicit Euler schemes. The project also investigates convergence and numerical stability.

**Grade: 5.85/6.0**

### Exercise 2 — Nonlinear Pendulum, Resonance and Chaos
Numerical study of a damped and externally driven pendulum, including nonlinear oscillations, resonance, sensitivity to initial conditions, chaotic dynamics, and Poincaré sections.

**Grade: 6.0/6.0**

### Exercise 3 — Gravitational Systems and Artemis II
Numerical simulation of one-, two-, and three-body gravitational systems using fourth-order Runge–Kutta methods and adaptive time stepping, including atmospheric re-entry, the Earth–Moon system, and Lagrange points.

**Grade: 6.0/6.0**

### Exercise 4 — Electrostatics and Numerical Boundary-Value Methods
Electrostatic modelling in cylindrical geometry with variable dielectric properties and free charges, using shooting methods, finite differences, and finite elements.

**Grade: 6.0/6.0**

### Exercise 5 — Wave Propagation in Inhomogeneous Media
Numerical solution of one-dimensional wave equations, including reflection, eigenmodes, resonance and stability, with an application to tsunami propagation over variable bathymetry.

**Grade: 6.0/6.0**

## Numerical Methods

- Explicit, implicit and semi-implicit Euler schemes
- Velocity-Verlet method
- Fourth-order Runge–Kutta (RK4)
- Adaptive time stepping
- Shooting method
- Finite differences
- Finite elements
- Eigenmode analysis
